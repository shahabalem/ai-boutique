\strategyPart{21 \textbar\ Methodology}{Research Methodology and Expected Contributions}

\section{Methodology}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Systematic literature review} — academic publications,
        consulting research, government frameworks, standards, company reports
        and regulatory publications.
  \item \textbf{Comparative framework analysis} — McKinsey, WEF, Deloitte,
        Stanford, NIST, OECD and academic models across strategy, technology,
        data, people, process, governance, measurement and structure.
  \item \textbf{Multiple-case study method} — organizations compared across
        industry, size and geography using a consistent template.
  \item \textbf{Expert interviews and employee surveys} — executives, AI
        leaders, product managers, engineers, compliance and HR.
  \item \textbf{Quantitative and qualitative analysis} — maturity--outcome
        correlations, cluster and factor analysis, thematic coding.
\end{itemize}

\section{Evidence hierarchy}

Audited financial/operational data and peer-reviewed studies rank above
official company reports, which rank above vendor-published case studies and
journalistic reports — and marketing claims are treated as the weakest
evidence. This protects the survey from treating vendor claims as verified
results.

\section{Expected contributions}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Theoretical} — an integrated framework connecting technology,
        organizational design, human work, governance and value.
  \item \textbf{Practical} — an AI maturity assessment, use-case prioritization
        model, operating-model options, workforce framework, governance
        checklist, case-study database and transformation roadmap.
  \item \textbf{Product management} — a defined role for AI product managers
        translating business problems into AI products with measurable
        adoption and outcomes.
\end{itemize}
